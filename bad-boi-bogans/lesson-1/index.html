Four algebra rules you actually need

Algebra is just careful bookkeeping for amounts you cannot name yet. In this lesson every symbol belongs to a real length of timber.

  1. =

    The equal sign is a promise

    Both sides contain the same total amount. It does not mean “the answer comes next.” Here, it is the seam where two equal lengths meet.

  2. x

    A letter is a missing number

    x, y, and z each stand for a timber length we do not know yet. The same letter always means the same length everywhere it appears.

  3. ↔

    Rearranging does not change the total

    You can split pieces apart, join them together, or move them around. Twelve inches must still be twelve inches when you finish.

  4. ?

    Use what you know to find what is missing

    Account for the labelled pieces first. The space left between them and the 12-inch master tells you the unknown length.

What just happened

The five teaching steps

Step 1

The Benchmark

The master timber beam is 12 inches long. Everything stacked underneath has to finish at that same 12-inch line.

Step 2

The Chaos & The Unknowns

The master remains intact while three matching work poles break into rows. Some length tags survive; the missing ones become x, y, and z. They are not magic textbook letters—just names for timber lengths you need to identify.

Step 3

Start With x

Row 1 already contains 6 inches. The remaining piece must also be 6 inches to reach 12, so x = 6. Every piece called x has that same length.

Step 4

The Domino Run

Row 3 becomes 4 + 6 + z = 12, leaving z = 2. Row 2 splits the full 12 inches into three equal cuts, so each y = 4.

Step 5

The Seam (=)

The equal sign is a two-way seam between arrangements containing the same amount. The pieces can unpack into 6 + 6 or fuse back into 12 without creating or destroying any timber.
